AI visibility snapshot · Canada · November 2026

Wondering how often AI is talking about Choice Hotels in Canada? We took a look.

We asked the questions Canadian travelers ask their AI assistants, with every answer filtered to Canada (Canadian location, English) across Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude, from national road-trip questions down to “hotels near Rogers Place.” We compared Choice against every brand AI named, with Best Western, Wyndham and IHG as tracked competitors, across 13 markets and 13 hotels. This AI Snapshot is an initial look at where things stand today and what a full GEO audit would explore further.

Why AI visibility matters

AI is becoming part of trip planning before a traveler ever reaches a booking site. When people ask where to stay in a city, near an attraction or along a highway, the hotels that surface in the answer are the ones that get considered, and often the ones that get booked.

01Where Choice stands

Where Choice stands in AI visibility today.

AI recommends Choice more than any other hotel brand in the Canadian markets we sampled. The room to grow is in “best hotels” questions, in how AI describes Choice next to its competitors, in the stories Choice already owns, and in the sources AI relies on.

#1

Choice is the most visible hotel brand in AI answers.

On questions that never mention Choice, any Choice brand or any Choice hotel, Choice appears in 24.2% of answers, ahead of Best Western at 16.9% and every other brand AI named. We sampled markets where Choice operates, which likely helps.

3%

“Best hotels” questions are the biggest gap.

Choice leads national, regional, city and neighborhood questions. When a traveler asks for the best hotels in an area, any Choice brand appears in 3.1% of answers. Fairmont leads at 23.1%.

61/100

AI describes Choice less warmly than its competitors.

Asked whether each Choice brand is good, AI averages 61/100, in the “room to improve” range, and only 3 of 11 brands reach 65. When travelers ask AI to compare, a competitor scored higher in all 12 comparisons we tracked.

The takeaway: Choice wins the broad questions. The next gains come from:
  • “Best hotels” content and reviews, where upscale brands lead today
  • Stories Choice already owns: the CFL partnership and pet-friendly hotels, in the markets where AI misses them
  • Sentiment: the reviews and content AI draws on when it describes Choice and compares it with competitors
  • Sources every engine uses: ChatGPT sends nearly 9 in 10 citations to choicehotels.com; Gemini and Claude mention Choice but rarely cite the site, so it needs to be more citable and listings more complete

08Technical quick audit

The site is open to AI crawlers. The opportunity is in what its pages say about location.

We spot-checked robots.txt, a hotel page, three city pages and three landmark pages, comparing the raw HTML a crawler receives with the page a visitor sees, plus part of a site crawl. choicehotels.com is a very large site, so this is a starting read: it needs a full technical audit of its own, which is part of the full GEO audit.

Crawler access

PASS
robots.txt explicitly allows OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, Claude-User and PerplexityBot, and does not block GPTBot, ClaudeBot or Google-Extended. The engine differences in section 04 are not an access problem.

Content citability

FAIL
AI cites choicehotels.com hotel pages, not the city and landmark pages built to answer “where should I stay” questions. Of the 25 choicehotels.com pages AI cited most, 16 are individual hotel pages and none is a city or landmark page. Those pages are hard to quote:
  • Landmark pages don’t name the landmark. The CN Tower page’s title and H1 say “290 Bremner Blvd” and never “CN Tower.”
  • Lists aren’t local. After the first hotel, the CN Tower list jumps to hotels 16 to 26 km away; the Toronto city page mixes in Mississauga, Oakville, Brampton and Markham.
  • Duplicate pages compete. The Air Canada Centre and Scotiabank Arena pages are identical and both point to themselves as the main version.
  • The answers travelers ask for are missing. City and hotel pages have no FAQ, and only one nearby attraction on the Comfort Inn Fallsview page has a walking time.

Rendering

PARTIAL
Hotel descriptions and amenities are in the raw HTML. City page headings and the distances on landmark pages only appear after JavaScript runs, so crawlers that read raw HTML miss them. The Comfort Inn Fallsview page is 3.6 MB of HTML for about 4,300 characters of visible text.

Structured data

PARTIAL
Hotel pages have strong Hotel markup (address, location and amenities such as EV charging, free hot breakfast and pools), but no ratings, price range or check-in time. City and landmark pages mark up each hotel by name only, with no address, so names like “Quality Inn & Suites” are ambiguous.

Internal links

PARTIAL
Sitemaps are declared and hotel pages point to clean main versions. A partial crawl surfaced malformed internal links (doubled slashes and external addresses appended to site paths), links that redirect to insecure http:// addresses, and broken links, including /faq.
Status reflects a limited sample; the full technical audit validates each finding sitewide.
09Quick wins

Three opportunities we can see before the full audit.

Each one comes straight from the findings above. The full audit validates and prioritizes them.

Quick win 01 · Owned content

Put real content on the pages AI already cites.

Of the 25 choicehotels.com pages AI cited most, 16 are individual hotel pages and the rest are the home, loyalty, deals and business-travel pages. None is a city or landmark page. Add what travelers ask about to the hotel pages AI reads (nearby landmarks with distances, pet policy, parking, breakfast, CFL game-day details), and give city and landmark pages content worth citing: name the landmark, put distances in the raw HTML, lead with nearby hotels, and add a short “best for” note and FAQ (see section 08). The full audit maps every page AI cites so new content goes where it expands what AI understands about Choice.

Quick win 02 · Earned authority

Make the listings and reviews Gemini and Claude trust tell the full Choice story.

Gemini’s top sources are Expedia, Kayak, Hotels.com and Booking.com; Claude’s are Facebook, Tripadvisor, Expedia and Booking.com. For the 13 sampled hotels, complete amenity details (pet-friendly, EV charging, pools, free breakfast), photos and neighborhood descriptions on those sites, and respond to reviews consistently, starting with Econo Lodge Downtown Ottawa and the Econo Lodge and Quality Inn brands. Both engines also cite BringFido for pet travel.

Quick win 03 · Stories Choice already owns

Close the pet-friendly and CFL gaps in Ottawa, Montreal and Edmonton.

AI named no Choice hotel for pet-friendly stays in Ottawa or Montreal, though Comfort Inn Ottawa East and Comfort Suites Downtown Montreal are on Choice’s own pet-friendly pages, and none near TD Place or Commonwealth Stadium (Edmonton). State the pet policy and the CFL partnership in plain text on those hotels’ pages and listings, and point game-day content at the hotels nearest each stadium.

Report methodology

This directional AI Snapshot uses 235 curated prompts (173 non-branded, 62 branded) run from Canada in English across Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode, Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude, using answers from November 12 to 13, 2026. Prompts are organized in layers: national (43), regional (7), city (58, including CFL game and pet-friendly questions), hotel area and named hotel (115), and brand comparisons (12), covering 13 markets with one sampled Choice hotel each. Non-branded prompts never mention Choice, any Choice brand or any Choice hotel; any prompt that does is treated as branded. Neighborhood, landmark, attraction, CFL, pet-friendly and brand reputation prompts were added on November 13 and reflect one run, so treat individual results for those questions as directional. The markets and hotel-area questions were chosen where Choice operates, which favors Choice; the full audit compares every market on equal terms. Brand rankings, engine rankings and share of voice use non-branded prompts only and include every brand the engines mentioned. Visibility is the share of answers that mention the brand; Choice’s figures count every Choice brand, while competitor brands are counted by the name AI used (so Marriott and Marriott Bonvoy, or Wyndham and its hotel brands, appear as separate rows). Sentiment is scored from 1 to 100 for each brand mentioned in an answer.

The technical spot check reviewed robots.txt and a small sample of choicehotels.com pages, comparing the raw HTML with the rendered page, plus part of a site crawl. A full engagement expands and validates the prompt universe, markets, languages, competitors, site templates, source and citation sets and technical evidence before recommendations are finalized. AI outputs and source selection change over time, so ongoing measurement is part of the GEO strategy.
